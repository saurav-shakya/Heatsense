## Executive summary

- **Government is the anchor buyer, and in 2026 heat finally got a dedicated disaster budget line.** The MHA added heatwaves and lightning to the notified-calamity list under the SDRF/NDRF operational guidelines for 2026–31, on the 16th Finance Commission's recommendation [1][2]. States had been capped at 10% of SDRF for "state-specific" heat relief [1][3]. Reported coverage says heat **mitigation** projects are also now eligible for Disaster Mitigation Funds [2]. That is the line item a forecasting and alerting tool fits under.
- **Heat Action Plans (HAPs) are everywhere, but most have no money.** Plans exist in 23 states, around 195 districts and 64 cities [5]. Of 37 HAPs that CPR reviewed, only 11 discussed funding sources, and 8 of those told departments to self-fund [6]. Delhi's HAP is run by DDMA and has been criticised for "inadequate funding" and "no legal teeth" [9][11].
- **Employers face soft rules, not hard ones.** The OSH Code 2020 is described as silent or vague on thermal stress [16]. Protection comes through advisories: MoLE/state labour commissioners [17][18] and NDMA's 2025 gig-worker advisory, which asks platforms to map shaded rest zones in-app and pause mandatory work from 11am to 4pm on orange/red days [19]. Platforms already spend on this: Swiggy runs 900+ "Recharge Zones" and Eternal (Zomato/Blinkit) has 4,500+ partner rest points [23]. This is the most commercially ready private buyer.
- **Insurance is the proven willingness-to-pay signal.** The SEWA / Climate Resilience for All (CRA) parametric product, carried by ICICI Lombard and reinsured by Swiss Re, paid Rs 2.84 crore to about 46,000 women in 2024 [26][27]. Members pay about $3.50 of an $8.23 premium [26]. Insurers need a trusted trigger and exposure data, which a ward-level heat model can supply.
- **Health could be the best government partner but is a weak payer.** NPCCHH runs daily heat-illness surveillance on IHIP from 1 March each year [34]. Suspected heatstroke cases rose from 4,481 (2022) to 48,385 (2024, through 28 July) [34]. This is a strong data-partnership and validation route, not a revenue line.

## 1. Government buyers

**Heat's disaster status and the funds it unlocks.** Down To Earth reported on 7 Aug 2026 that MHA included heatwaves and lightning as notified natural calamities under the SDRF/NDRF guidelines for 2026–31 [1]. Before that, states had to notify heat at state level and could spend at most 10% of their annual SDRF allocation on it [1]. Telangana's April 2025 state-specific notification used exactly that route and paid Rs 4 lakh ex-gratia per death from SDRF [3]. Secondary reporting says the change came via the 16th FC, that NDRF tops up SDRF when state funds run short, and that heatwave mitigation projects can now draw on Disaster Mitigation Funds [2]. The same source notes the change is operational finance, not an amendment to the DM Act [2]. Under the 15th FC (2021–26), the State Disaster Mitigation Fund received about Rs 32,031 crore (20% of state disaster corpus) and the National Disaster Mitigation Fund about Rs 13,693 crore [4]. The 16th FC (2026–31) amounts were not in the results. **Action:** get the MHA 2026–31 SDRF/SDMF guidelines and check whether "early warning systems/IT" is a permitted mitigation expense.

**NDMA guidelines and HAP coverage.** NDMA issued heat guidelines in 2016 and revised them in 2017 and 2019. In 2019 it named 23 heat-prone states [5]. A recent MoHUA figure puts HAPs at 23 states, 195 districts and 64 cities, and these jurisdictions overlap [5]. An NDMA 2025 presentation says "23 States, 200+ districts and Cities" [5].

**Funding reality.** CPR's 2023 review of 37 HAPs found only 11 named funding sources, 8 of which relied on departments funding themselves. Only 10 had local thresholds and only 2 had done vulnerability assessments [6]. That last gap is the one HeatShield fills: ward-level, localised thresholds. Climate Policy Initiative says there is no estimate of public spending on heat resilience. Its estimate of 2021 losses is about 167 bn labour hours and roughly $159 bn, 5.4% of GDP [31].

**City budgets.**
- *Ahmedabad:* AMC put INR 56,194 million (about Rs 5,619 crore) toward climate/sustainability in its 2025–26 budget. This is India's first dedicated climate budget chapter, but it is not heat-specific [7]. CDKN funded the original HAP work with GBP 300,000 [8].
- *Delhi:* The HAP 2025 was launched on 21 Apr 2025 by CM Rekha Gupta and designed with NDMA [9]. It includes IMD colour alerts, heat wards in hospitals, 3,000 water ATMs, 1,800 Aapda Mitras, and IDSP tracking of heat illness [9]. DDMA publishes the plan: HAP 2024-25 and a 21-04-2025 version [9]. The red-alert trigger is at least 6°C above normal [9]. Critics cite "inadequate funding" and "almost no ground-level execution" [11], and bus shelters promised in the previous year that were never built [10]. A 2026 update adds a Cool Roof Policy and focuses on "thermal hotspots" [12]. That points toward hotspot or ward targeting, but no budget figure was found [12].
- *Nagpur:* An early HAP city (case data reportedly fell from 438 in 2016 to 176 in 2022). In March 2026 NMC had still not finalised that year's HAP [13]. This shows how much execution depends on the commissioner.

**Smart City ICCCs as the delivery channel.** Command centres run real-time monitoring of traffic, safety and water, and some act as disaster platforms [14]. Typical costs: Chennai approved Rs 98.25 crore for 5-year ICCC operations plus "ICCC 2.0" [14], and Amritsar's ICCC cost Rs 91 crore [14]. No source confirmed heat as a standard ICCC module [14]. That is a gap, and a natural integration point via an "ICCC 2.0" add-on.

**Chief Heat Officers.** The Atlantic Council says it supported eight CHOs worldwide, including in India, but the results did not name the city [15]. NDMA's 2025 international heat workshop advocated for CHOs in Indian cities [15]. No confirmed Indian municipal CHO post was found. Treat the role as emerging.

## 2. Employers

**Regulation.** Commentary on the new labour codes says the OSH Code 2020 "remains vague on safeguards for workers working in thermal stress." Unions say the codes do not specify protections for extreme-weather days, which leaves it to central notifications [16]. In practice, protection comes through advisories. The 2023 MoLE advisory, relayed by the Haryana Labour Commissioner, asked for rescheduled hours, drinking water and similar steps [17]. Kerala made a 12–3pm rest period mandatory for outdoor workers in Feb 2025, with an 8-hour day between 7am and 7pm [18]. J&K issued an advisory for the 12–3pm window covering construction sites and brick kilns [18]. NDMA's July 2025 advisory for gig and informal workers covers 15-minute breaks every 90 minutes, rest zones mapped in-app, 2 L water per shift, and work restrictions on alert days. Experts say it is not legally binding and enforcement is "almost non-existent" [19]. The Factories Act section text was not retrieved.

**Workforce size.**
- **Construction:** about 71 M workers in 2023 per Knight Frank–RICS, the second-largest employer, 81% unskilled, projected to reach 100 M [20]. Older estimates range from 51 M to 57.5 M [20]. The Building and Other Construction Workers (BOCW) welfare cess (1% of construction cost) collected Rs 78,521 crore and about Rs 43,122 crore of it is unspent [21]. That is a large, under-used state labour-welfare pot that could fund worker heat alerts.
- **Gig/platform:** 7.7 M in 2020–21, rising to 23.5 M by 2029–30 (NITI Aayog, June 2022) [22].
- **Heat-exposed labour overall:** about 75% of the workforce, roughly 380 M people, per a 2022 World Bank estimate (via secondary reporting) [36].

**Platform heat programmes (the buyer signal).** Swiggy runs 900+ climate-controlled Recharge Zones. Eternal partners with 4,500+ restaurants for rest areas. Blinkit gives out 1.5 lakh glucose sachets a day. Zomato's "weather union" sends preemptive heatwave alerts [23]. These firms already build alerting and rest-point mapping in-house. HeatShield's opening is ward-level risk data or an API sold to them, plus a compliance report against the NDMA advisory, rather than a worker-facing app.

**ESG/BRSR.** BRSR Core includes "enhancing employee wellbeing and safety" (wellbeing spend as a share of revenue, and safety incidents) as essential indicators. It phases in with assurance, from the top 150 in FY24 to the top 1,000 by FY27 [24]. Heat is not named explicitly. A heat-safety log could become supporting evidence for those KPIs, especially for value-chain disclosures in construction, logistics and quick-commerce.

## 3. Insurers and finance

**SEWA heat product.**
- *2023 pilot:* about 21,000 women in 5 Gujarat districts. Arsht-Rock covered the premium, about $10.30 per person. The trigger was a 3-day accumulated satellite temperature (134–138°C total in Ahmedabad). The payout cap was about $85 and the expected average payout about $28 (Blue Marble design) [25]. Reports say it made no payouts because the thresholds were too high [26].
- *2024:* about 50,000 women across 22 districts in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Maharashtra. It triggered in 17 districts and paid 46,339 women Rs 151–1,651 each, plus a Rs 400 cash layer in all 22 districts, for a total of Rs 2.84 crore [26]. Members paid about $3.50 and CRA paid about $4.73 [26]. ICICI Lombard is the local insurer and Swiss Re the reinsurer [27].
- *2025:* reported scale-up to 250,000 women in 34 districts, with cyclone and rain cover added [27]. This is single-source and unverified.

**Regulator and government.** Reuters (Oct 2025) reported early talks with insurers on a nationwide parametric climate scheme with temperature among the triggers. No formal proposal existed and IRDAI declined to comment [28]. No IRDAI sandbox approval for heat was found.

**DFIs and climate funds.** A proposed World Bank loan of $350 M (P508719) for affordable cooling devices through DPIIT, board date 2027 [29]. Tamil Nadu's Heat Resilience Centre is UK-backed with UNEP and WRI, runs to Sept 2026, and is building "bankable projects" for development banks [30]. No ADB or GCF heat-specific India project was found [29].

**Philanthropy.** Rockefeller gave IISER Pune $499,200 (Nov 2025–Nov 2027) for heat-risk thresholds and early-warning decision tools [32]. Rockefeller and Wellcome committed $11.5 M to South Asia climate-health early warning, announced at Mumbai Climate Week in Feb 2026 [32]. Bloomberg Philanthropies and CIFF fund C40, which issued an RfP for a Bengaluru heat-and-health protocol [33]. These are grant or pilot buyers, useful for funding Delhi validation but not recurring revenue.

## 4. Healthcare

NPCCHH and NCDC require daily IHIP reporting of heatstroke cases and deaths from 1 March, with patient-level clinical data from 2025, a 5-day correction window, and IMD 4-day forecasts passed to facilities [34]. Suspected heatstroke cases: 65 (2021), 4,481 (2022), 19,402 (2023), 48,385 (2024 through 28 Jul). Confirmed deaths: 33, 189 and 185 for 2022–24 [34]. In 2024 the Centre told central hospitals to set up special heatwave units [35]. **Implication:** NPCCHH is a natural partner for co-validating ward alerts against IHIP case data and for an MoU, and state health departments could pay for hospital surge forecasting from NHM budgets. No priced precedent was found.

**Price-point anchors (weak).** GeM municipal software bids are small, for example Rs 5.54 lakh in Kolkata, and reverse auctions cut prices by about 25% [37]. A CAG audit found municipalities implemented only 5–6 of 18 computerised functions [37]. ICCC O&M contracts run around Rs 20 crore a year (Chennai: Rs 98 crore over 5 years) [14]. **Inference, not sourced:** a stand-alone municipal heat SaaS probably clears Rs 5–25 lakh a year. Larger tickets would come through an ICCC system integrator or a state SDMA/SDMF project. The sources gave no B2G sales-cycle data. Practitioners' rule of thumb is 9–18 months, aligned to the Feb–March pre-season. Note that NMC had still not finalised its HAP by mid-March 2026 [13].

## 6. Precedents

- **Skymet:** sells weather and crop analytics to insurers, banks, agribusiness and the public sector. It raised a ~$10 M Series C from BlueOrchard / InsuResilience [39]. It owns its weather stations and states pay rental over long contracts, for example Odisha WINDS under PMFBY, with state share paid from the PMFBY budget [39]. **Lesson:** attach to a funded central scheme (here, SDRF/SDMF heat mitigation) and sell infrastructure-as-a-service to states.
- **Blue Sky Analytics:** satellite environmental data sold as an API or SaaS to B2B and government. It raised about $1.2 M seed and reported FY25 revenue of only Rs 5.7 lakh [38]. **Lesson:** selling climate data to Indian buyers without an anchor scheme gave it very little revenue.
- **Vassar Labs:** decision-support systems for state water and cyclone management (Andhra Pradesh) and for NITI Aayog and the World Bank [40]. **Lesson:** a project-based B2G model, often funded by DFIs.
- **CropIn:** enterprise SaaS sold to agribusinesses by licence or acreage. With governments, DFIs and CSR it uses a "collaborative" model. About 60% of revenue is international [41]. **Lesson:** private enterprise customers pay recurring fees, while government work is partnership-style.
